# Reviewer Attacks

## Attack 1: This is just force closure.

Response: the mathematics is force closure. The contribution is the task-conditioned generator set: delete forbidden role generators before certificate search.

## Attack 2: Semantic grasping already handles task roles.

Response: semantic grasping can propose or rank contacts. SFC audits whether the active force-closure witness uses only task-legal roles.

## Attack 3: The benchmark is synthetic and adversarial.

Response: accepted. The paper is a synthetic mechanism paper. Negative controls show when the gap disappears.

## Attack 4: Geometric FC true-legal rate of 0.000 is too extreme.

Response: the trap-heavy protocol makes forbidden contacts mechanically attractive. Family C shows top-k posthoc can recover legal witnesses as it searches more, which supports the witness-ordering claim.

## Attack 5: SFC assumes perfect labels.

Response: oracle SFC is a mechanism reference. Family B and G explicitly show observed SFC can be unsafe under role-label noise.

## Attack 6: Risk-aware SFC is not generally safe.

Response: correct. The paper claims only the calibrated threshold-0.70 slice result and states that overconfident wrong labels remain a deployment threat.

## Attack 7: No learned planner baseline.

Response: this is not a grasp proposal benchmark. Learned planners can provide candidate contacts; SFC is a certificate layer over those contacts.

## Attack 8: No hardware.

Response: correct. The paper does not claim real-robot performance.
